# PB-02 Pumping Station Power Loss

## 1. Purpose and scope
How to keep drinking water booster stations and wastewater lift stations running when utility power is lost or is expected to be lost.

## 2. Triggers
- A pumping station's feeding substation is rated High or Critical.
- SCADA reports loss of utility power at a pumping station.
- A field crew reports flooding in a wet well or control panel.

## 3. Stations with standby generators
1. Confirm the generator started and transferred load (SCADA or site visit).
2. Check fuel level; request a refuel when below 12 hours of run time.
3. Fuel priority: stations serving hospitals first, then stations serving shelters, then the rest.

## 4. Stations without standby generators
1. Before landfall, deliver and connect a portable generator to every High or Critical station that serves a hospital, shelter or nursing home.
2. If no generator is available, lift stations can be pumped down by vacuum truck (bypass pumping) every 4–6 hours.
3. Booster stations without power reduce pressure in their zone. If pressure falls below 20 psi, follow PB-03 §5 (precautionary boil water notice).

## 5. Flooding
1. Do not operate a station with water in the control panel. Isolate power and wait for the water to recede.
2. Submerged pumps and motors must be inspected and megger-tested before restart.

## 6. Communication
- Notify the Water Operations supervisor for the region within 15 minutes of a station going down.
- If a lift station overflows, Regulatory Affairs must report the sanitary sewer overflow to Florida DEP within 24 hours (see PB-04 §6).

## 7. Known issues
- PS-007 (Fort Myers South Booster): standby generator failed its June 2022 load test and was removed; portable generator hookup only. Serves Caloosa Regional Medical Center and the South Fort Myers High School shelter.
- PS-002 (Davis Islands Lift): running on a single pump pending impeller repair.
- PS-016 (Fort Myers Beach Lift): at 1 m elevation, 50 m from the shoreline; floods in any surge above 1 m.
