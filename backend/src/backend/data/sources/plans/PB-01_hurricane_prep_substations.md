# PB-01 Hurricane Preparation — Substations and Transmission

## 1. Purpose and scope
Actions to protect substations, transmission lines and generation sites before a tropical storm or hurricane, and to restore them safely afterwards. Applies to all SGW power assets in the North, Central and South regions.

## 2. Activation
- Activate when an SGW region is inside the NHC 5-day cone, or a Tropical Storm Watch or Hurricane Watch covers any part of the service area.
- The Emergency Manager on duty declares activation and opens the storm event. T&D supervisors confirm receipt within 1 hour.

## 3. Pre-landfall actions (T-72 h to T-24 h)
1. Review the risk list for High and Critical substations; walk down every Critical site in person.
2. Low-lying sites (flood zone AE or VE, or control house below 3 m elevation): install flood barriers or sandbags around control houses and relay panels; raise portable equipment off the floor.
3. Confirm battery banks and relay protection are healthy; replace any battery bank that failed its last test.
4. Transmission corridors with open vegetation work orders: patrol and clear hazard trees where time allows. Log any corridor that cannot be cleared.
5. Pre-position mobile transformers and spare breakers at inland staging yards (Lakeland, Arcadia).
6. Request mutual aid crews through the regional mutual assistance group when two or more Critical substations are forecast.

## 4. Final actions (T-24 h to landfall)
1. Stop all non-emergency switching and planned work.
2. Crews move to shelter once sustained winds reach 40 mph (35 kt). No bucket truck work above 30 mph.
3. For coastal substations forecast to flood (surge above control house floor), consider pre-emptive de-energisation to prevent equipment damage. Requires T&D supervisor and Emergency Manager approval.

## 5. Post-landfall damage assessment
1. Begin assessment when sustained winds drop below 40 mph.
2. Order: transmission lines and bulk substations first, then distribution substations feeding hospitals, water plants and shelters, then the rest by customers served.
3. Do not re-energise a substation that was flooded until relays and switchgear are inspected and dried.

## 6. Restoration priority
1. Bulk transmission and generation.
2. Substations feeding hospitals, emergency operations centres and water/wastewater treatment plants.
3. Substations feeding shelters and nursing homes.
4. Remaining substations by number of customers.

## 7. Known weak points (update each season)
- TL-008 (Lee Bulk – Fort Myers South): deferred crossarm replacement and vegetation encroachment, spans 14–19.
- SUB-017 (Fort Myers Beach): switchgear below design flood elevation.
- SUB-014 (Fort Myers South): single 69 kV supply via TL-008; transformer T2 under temporary repair.
