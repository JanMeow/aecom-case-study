# PB-05 Storm Surge and Flooding — Low-Lying Assets

## 1. Purpose and scope
Storm surge causes most catastrophic equipment damage at coastal sites. This playbook covers assets in FEMA flood zones AE and VE, or below 3 m elevation.

## 2. Surge thresholds
- Surge depth at a site = forecast surge minus site elevation.
- Above 0 m: expect water in yards and cable trenches.
- Above 0.5 m: expect water in control houses and pump rooms; plan to de-energise.
- Above 1 m: assume electrical equipment is lost; stage replacements.

## 3. Pre-landfall actions
1. Identify every site where forecast surge exceeds site elevation (use the NHC storm surge forecast and the GIS elevation field).
2. Remove portable electronics and spare parts to higher ground.
3. Install flood barriers where available; sandbag doors and cable entries.
4. Plan controlled shutdown of equipment that would be damaged by energised flooding.

## 4. Access
- Record which access roads are expected to flood. Crews may not reach barrier-island sites (e.g. Fort Myers Beach) for 24–72 hours after landfall.
- Pre-stage crews on the mainland side of bridges.

## 5. After the water recedes
1. No re-energisation until the site is inspected, dried and tested.
2. Photograph damage for insurance and FEMA Public Assistance claims before cleanup.
3. Record the high-water mark at each flooded site to improve future elevation data.

## 6. Past surge damage
- Hurricane Irma (2017): SUB-017 and PS-016 flooded; SUB-014 relay failure.
- Tropical Storm Eta (2020): PS-002, PS-005 and SUB-005 flooded in Tampa Bay.
