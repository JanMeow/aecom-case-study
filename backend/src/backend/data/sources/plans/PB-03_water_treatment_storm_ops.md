# PB-03 Water and Wastewater Treatment — Storm Operations

## 1. Purpose and scope
Keeping drinking water safe and wastewater contained at SGW treatment plants during and after a hurricane. Supports SGW's AWIA risk and resilience assessment and emergency response plan.

## 2. Pre-landfall (T-72 h to T-24 h)
1. Fill all clear wells and elevated storage tanks to maximum.
2. Top up standby generator fuel to full; confirm the fuel supplier's storm delivery commitment.
3. Stock chemicals (chlorine, coagulant) for at least 7 days.
4. Wastewater plants: lower basin levels where possible to create storage for storm inflow.

## 3. Staffing
- Each treatment plant stays staffed through the storm with a ride-out crew of at least two certified operators and supplies for 72 hours.

## 4. Loss of utility power
1. Confirm transfer to standby generation. A plant's run time is limited by its on-site fuel (see GIS `backup_hours`).
2. When fuel falls below 12 hours, escalate to the Emergency Manager for priority refuelling.
3. If a plant's feeding substation is expected to stay out longer than its fuel lasts, ask T&D to raise that substation's restoration priority (PB-01 §6).

## 5. Precautionary boil water notice
1. Issue a precautionary boil water notice when distribution pressure drops below 20 psi in any zone, or treatment is interrupted.
2. Regulatory Affairs notifies the county health department; Communications publishes the notice (PB-04 §5).
3. Lift the notice only after two consecutive days of satisfactory bacteriological samples.

## 6. Wastewater overflow
1. If a plant or lift station overflows, contain where possible, and record start time, location and estimated volume.
2. Report to Florida DEP within 24 hours (PB-04 §6).

## 7. Restoration
1. Restore treatment plants before booster stations; there is no pressure to restore without treated water.
2. Flush and sample any zone that lost pressure before lifting a boil water notice.
